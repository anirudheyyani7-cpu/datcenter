# New Entrant Playbook

## Pattern Recognition
When new capital sources like [[Arka Ventures]] enter the [[Data Center Market]], they follow recognizable patterns for success or failure. This playbook captures proven approaches.

## Phase 1: Foundation (Months 0-6)

### Build Knowledge Base
- Engage consultants to compress learning curve
- Attend industry conferences ([[DCD]], [[7x24 Exchange]])
- Build relationships with [[Hyperscale Tenants]]
- Study market dynamics and power constraints

### Establish Credibility
- Announce capital commitment publicly
- Hire recognized industry executives
- Join industry associations
- Launch [[JV Partner Diligence Process]]

## Phase 2: First Project (Months 6-18)

### Partnership Selection
- Prioritize proven [[JV Partners]] over optimal economics
- Focus on partners with existing [[Hyperscale]] relationships
- Accept minority position to gain experience
- Structure for knowledge transfer and governance rights

### Market Selection
- Target [[Tier-3 Markets]] with available [[Power Infrastructure]]
- Avoid overheated markets where established players dominate
- Prioritize speed over perfect location
- Secure multiple sites to maintain optionality

## Phase 3: Scale (Months 18-36)

### Build Internal Capabilities
- Transition from 100% partnered to hybrid model
- Recruit technical team (power, design, development)
- Establish direct relationships with utilities
- Develop proprietary [[Site Selection]] methodology

### Expand Geographic Footprint
- Diversify across 3-4 primary markets
- Establish regional development platforms
- Build [[Power Pipeline]] ahead of site acquisition

## Success Metrics by Phase

### Year 1
- JV partner secured
- First site under control
- Power allocation letter obtained
- Initial pre-leasing progress

### Year 2
- Construction commenced on first project
- Second and third projects in development
- Internal team established
- Direct hyperscale relationships developed

### Year 3
- First project operational
- 500+ MW in development pipeline
- Mix of partnered and direct projects
- Recognized brand in target markets

## Common Failure Modes
- Attempting direct development without expertise
- Underestimating power procurement timeline
- Over-optimizing first deal vs moving quickly
- Insufficient capital commitment relative to ambition
- Lack of focus across too many markets
