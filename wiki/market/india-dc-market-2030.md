# India DC Market Outlook to 2030

## Market Size Projection

The India data center market is projected to reach **$22 Billion by 2030**, representing a massive expansion from current levels of approximately $4-5 Billion (2024).

## Growth Drivers

### 1. Hyperscale Cloud Expansion
- [[AWS]], [[Microsoft Azure]], [[Google Cloud]] increasing regional presence
- Data localization requirements driving in-country builds
- [[AI Infrastructure]] and [[GPU Clusters]] requiring new capacity

### 2. Digital India Initiatives
- Government push for digital services
- 5G rollout creating edge computing demand
- Smart city projects requiring distributed infrastructure

### 3. Enterprise Digital Transformation
- Financial services modernization
- Manufacturing IoT adoption
- Healthcare data requirements

## Supply Constraints

Despite demand, growth is limited by:

### Power Availability
- [[Tier-1 Cities]] face grid capacity constraints
- State [[DISCOMs]] struggle with allocation
- **150MW allocations** (like [[Brookfield]]'s target) are significant undertakings

### Land Scarcity
- Prime locations near fiber meet points are limited
- Zoning restrictions in metros
- Environmental clearances take 12-18 months

### Skilled Labor
- DC operations expertise is thin
- Competition for talent with global operators

## Market Structure Evolution

**Current (2024)**:
- Dominated by domestic players: [[CtrlS]], [[Yotta Infrastructure]], [[Netmagic]]
- Limited hyperscale presence
- ~1,200MW total capacity

**Projected (2030)**:
- Global operators capturing 40-50% share
- Hyperscale facilities in 6-8 cities
- ~6,000-8,000MW capacity
- [[Institutional Capital]] dominance

## Geographic Distribution

### Tier-1 Markets (60% of capacity)
- [[Mumbai]] - Financial + subsea cables
- [[Chennai]] - Southern hub + manufacturing
- [[Bangalore]] - Technology ecosystem
- [[Hyderabad]] - Emerging hyperscale destination

### Tier-2 Growth Markets (30%)
- [[Pune]], [[Kolkata]], [[Delhi NCR]]
- Lower costs, improving connectivity

### Tier-3 / Edge (10%)
- Distributed for latency-sensitive applications
- 5G-driven deployments

## Investment Implications

For institutional investors like [[Brookfield Asset Management]]:

- **Entry timing**: 2024-2026 is critical window before market matures
- **Scale matters**: Need 100MW+ to achieve economies
- **Power is the moat**: Securing allocation creates competitive advantage
- **Exit multiples**: Early entrants can sell to core infrastructure buyers at premium

## Risk Factors

- Regulatory changes in data sovereignty
- Power tariff inflation
- Competition from Southeast Asia (Singapore, Malaysia)
- Slower-than-expected hyperscale adoption
