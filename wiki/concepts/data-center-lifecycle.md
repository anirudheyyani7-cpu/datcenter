# Data Center Lifecycle Investment Phases

Investors targeting data center infrastructure must select entry points along the asset lifecycle, each offering distinct risk-return profiles.

## Phase 1: Greenfield Development

[[Greenfield Development]] involves ground-up construction on raw land:

**Characteristics**:
- 24-36 month development timeline
- Highest capital intensity
- Maximum design control and efficiency
- Pre-leasing risk during construction
- Permitting and utility interconnection complexity

**Return Profile**: 15-25% IRR potential with full lease-up

**Best For**: Institutional investors with development expertise and patient capital

## Phase 2: Brownfield Expansion

[[Brownfield Expansion]] adds capacity to existing facilities:

**Characteristics**:
- 12-18 month timeline
- Existing utility infrastructure
- Proven market demand
- Customer expansion opportunities
- Lower permitting risk

**Return Profile**: 12-18% IRR with moderate risk

**Best For**: Operators seeking to scale with existing customers

## Phase 3: Stabilized Asset Acquisition

Purchasing fully-leased, operating data centers:

**Characteristics**:
- Immediate cash flow
- Lower returns (8-12% IRR)
- Tenant credit risk vs. development risk
- Limited value-add potential
- Easier financing

**Best For**: Core infrastructure funds, REITs seeking yield

## Phase 4: Platform Acquisition

Acquiring operating companies with multiple assets and development pipelines:

**Characteristics**:
- Hybrid risk-return (10-18% IRR)
- Management team and relationships included
- Portfolio diversification
- Built-in growth pipeline
- Operational expertise transfer

**Best For**: Funds seeking scale and market entry like [[Brookfield Asset Management]]

## Emerging Phase 5: Edge Infrastructure

[[Edge Data Centers]] represent a newer lifecycle category with:
- Smaller facility sizes (1-5MW)
- Distributed geographic deployment
- Telecom partnership models
- 5G and IoT demand drivers
