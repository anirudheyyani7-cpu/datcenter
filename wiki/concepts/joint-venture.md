# Joint Venture Strategy

## Overview
A partnership structure where two or more parties collaborate on data center development, sharing capital, expertise, risks, and returns.

## When JV Makes Sense

### Ideal Candidate Profile
- **New Market Entrants:** Limited track record (like [[Arka Ventures]])
- **Capital Constrained:** Want exposure without full project financing
- **Risk Mitigation:** Prefer shared downside
- **Knowledge Gap:** Need operational partner expertise

### Typical JV Structures

**Developer + Capital Partner:**
- Developer brings land, permits, relationships
- Capital partner provides financing
- Split: typically 20-40% developer, 60-80% capital

**Operator + Investor:**
- Operator brings technical and commercial expertise
- Investor provides equity capital
- Long-term operational economics sharing

**Geographic Expansion:**
- Established operator in Region A
- Local partner in Region B
- Shared market entry risks

## Advantages
- Reduced capital requirements
- Shared technical and market risk
- Access to partner expertise and relationships
- Faster market entry
- Learning opportunity for new entrants

## Disadvantages
- Shared upside
- Governance complexity
- Potential partner conflicts
- Exit complexity

## Best Practices
- Clear governance documentation
- Defined exit mechanisms
- Aligned incentives
- Complementary capabilities
