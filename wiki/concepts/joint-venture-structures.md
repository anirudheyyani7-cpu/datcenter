# Joint Venture Structures in Datacenter Development

## Overview

A **Joint Venture (JV)** in datacenter development is a strategic partnership where two or more parties collaborate to develop, own, and/or operate datacenter facilities while sharing risks, resources, and rewards.

## Why JVs for New Entrants

For [[Greenfield Client]]s like [[Notion]], JVs offer critical advantages:

- **Risk Distribution**: Capital and operational risks shared across partners
- **Expertise Access**: Immediate access to experienced operators and technical teams
- **Market Credibility**: Association with established datacenter players
- **Operational Support**: Ongoing management from partner with proven track record
- **Faster Time-to-Market**: Leverage partner's existing processes and relationships

## Common JV Structures

### 1. Development JV
- Partners collaborate on facility design and construction
- Typically 50/50 or 60/40 equity splits
- One partner (operator) usually has operational control
- New entrant provides capital, experienced partner provides expertise

### 2. Operating JV
- Facility owned by one party, operated jointly
- Fee-based or profit-sharing arrangements
- Lower capital commitment for new entrant
- Builds operational capability over time

### 3. Platform JV
- Long-term partnership across multiple facilities
- Standardized processes and governance
- Economies of scale as portfolio grows

## Key Structural Elements

- **Governance**: Board composition, voting rights, veto powers
- **Capital Contributions**: Initial investment and future funding obligations
- **Profit Distribution**: Waterfall structures, preferred returns
- **Exit Mechanisms**: Buy-sell provisions, tag-along rights
- **Operational Control**: Decision-making authority for day-to-day operations

## Success Criteria

1. **Aligned Objectives**: Both partners seeking similar outcomes
2. **Clear Roles**: Well-defined responsibilities and authorities
3. **Cultural Fit**: Compatible working styles and values
4. **Transparent Communication**: Regular reporting and joint planning
5. **Fair Economics**: Balanced risk-reward allocation
