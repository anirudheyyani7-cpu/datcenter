# Minority Stakes Strategy in Data Centers

Minority stake investments represent a distinct approach to data center investing, offering unique advantages for both investors and operating platforms.

## Structure Overview

A minority stake typically involves acquiring **less than 50%** of equity ownership, allowing:
- Operating team to retain control
- Investor to participate in upside without operational responsibility
- Lower capital commitment than majority buyouts
- Partnership dynamics rather than owner-operator relationship

## Investor Benefits

### Capital Efficiency
Minority positions require less capital per investment, enabling portfolio diversification across multiple platforms, geographies, or development stages.

### Risk Mitigation
- Reduced exposure to single-platform execution risk
- Ability to exit or reduce position more easily
- Less liability for operational issues
- No need for internal operating expertise

### Strategic Access
- Partnership with proven management teams
- First look at future funding rounds
- Board observation or participation rights
- Information rights for market intelligence

## Operator Benefits

### Capital Without Control Loss
Developers can access growth capital while maintaining decision-making authority and operational control.

### Validation and Credibility
Institutional minority investors provide market validation, improving access to debt financing and customer confidence.

### Strategic Partnership
Beyond capital, minority investors often provide:
- Customer introductions
- Market insights
- M&A advisory
- Follow-on capital access

## Common Structures

- Preferred equity with liquidation preferences
- Common equity with board rights
- Convertible instruments
- Joint ventures at project level

## Market Context

Minority stakes have become increasingly common in [[data center]] investing as platforms seek growth capital without relinquishing control, and investors seek exposure without operational burden.
