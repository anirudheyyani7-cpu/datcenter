# Secondary Transactions in Data Centers

Secondary transactions represent the sale of existing stakes in operating data center platforms, as opposed to [[Greenfield Development]] or [[Primary Capital Raises]].

## Transaction Structure

Secondary transactions typically involve:
- Sale of ownership stakes by existing investors or sponsors
- Operating assets with established cash flows and customers
- Proven management teams and operational track records
- Existing [[Colocation]] or [[Hyperscale]] customer relationships

## Investor Appeal

**For Buyers**: Secondary transactions offer several advantages:
- Reduced execution risk compared to [[Greenfield]]
- Immediate cash flow generation
- Established market position and brand
- Operating history for underwriting
- Existing [[Power]] and [[Fiber Connectivity]] infrastructure

**Risk Considerations**:
- Higher entry valuations than greenfield
- Limited upside if platform already scaled
- Inherited operational issues or legacy contracts
- Competitive auction processes driving prices

## Market Context

Secondary transaction volume has increased significantly as:
- Early-stage PE investors seek exits after 3-5 year hold periods
- Founders and family offices pursue liquidity events
- Strategic buyers consolidate fragmented markets
- [[Institutional Investors]] like [[Blackstone Credit]] seek scaled platforms
