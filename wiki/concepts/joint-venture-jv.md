# Joint Venture (JV) in Datacenter Development

## Definition
A Joint Venture (JV) is a strategic partnership structure where two or more parties collaborate to develop, own, and operate [[datacenter infrastructure]], sharing capital investment, operational expertise, risk, and returns.

## When JV Is Recommended

**Ideal Client Profiles**:
- [[New market entrants]] with capital but limited operational experience
- Investors with readiness scores in the 60-80 range
- Clients seeking to minimize execution risk
- Organizations wanting to leverage existing [[datacenter operators]]

**Market Conditions Favoring JV**:
- High-barrier markets requiring local partnerships
- [[Tier-3]] and [[emerging markets]] with regulatory complexity
- Markets where land acquisition or power access requires local connections

## JV Structure Models

**Equity Partnership**:
- 50/50 or customized equity splits
- One partner provides capital, other provides operational expertise
- Shared governance and decision-making

**Build-Operate-Transfer (BOT)**:
- JV builds the facility
- Operating partner manages for defined period
- Eventual transfer to capital partner

**Operator-Investor Model**:
- Established [[datacenter operator]] partners with financial investor
- Operator contributes expertise, brand, customers
- Investor provides development capital

## Advantages
- **Risk Mitigation**: Shared financial and execution risk
- **Expertise Access**: Leverage partner's operational capabilities
- **Market Entry**: Faster entry through established relationships
- **Capital Efficiency**: Lower individual capital requirements

## Challenges
- Governance complexity and potential conflicts
- Exit strategy alignment
- Profit-sharing negotiations
- Cultural and operational integration
