# Greenfield Client

## Definition

A **Greenfield Client** is an organization entering the datacenter industry without prior operational experience, existing facilities, or established infrastructure. These clients start from a blank slate ("green field") and require comprehensive guidance across all aspects of datacenter development.

## Characteristics

- No existing datacenter portfolio
- Limited or no in-house technical expertise
- Minimal industry relationships
- High learning curve requirements
- Often from adjacent industries (tech, real estate, finance)

## Risk Profile

Greenfield clients present elevated risk due to:
- **Knowledge gaps**: Unfamiliarity with technical requirements
- **Operational inexperience**: No track record managing datacenters
- **Market misunderstanding**: Potential unrealistic expectations
- **Capital inefficiency**: Higher likelihood of cost overruns

## Recommended Strategies

### Joint Venture Approach
Most suitable for greenfield clients like [[Notion]]. Benefits include:
- Risk sharing with experienced partners
- Knowledge transfer opportunities
- Faster market credibility
- Operational support from day one

### Alternative Pathways
- **Acquisition**: Purchase existing facility to gain immediate operational presence
- **Build-to-Suit**: Partner with developer who retains operational responsibility
- **Management Services**: Own asset but outsource operations to specialist

## Success Factors

1. **Realistic timeline expectations** (24-36 months typical)
2. **Committed learning investment**
3. **Strong capital reserves** (15-20% contingency recommended)
4. **Quality partner selection**
5. **Phased approach** to capacity deployment

## Common Pitfalls

- Underestimating complexity
- Inadequate due diligence on partners
- Insufficient operational planning
- Overambitious initial scope
